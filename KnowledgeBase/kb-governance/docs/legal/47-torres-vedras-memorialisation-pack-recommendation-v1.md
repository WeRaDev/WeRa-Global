# Torres Vedras — Pilot Memorialisation Pack

> Question being answered: *"What should we ask the pilot customer to sign in order to finish the commercialisation part of the pilot and move to the next step?"*

## 1. Framing — what "finishing the commercialisation part" actually means here

The temptation is to interpret the question as: "draft a 20-year `fixed service fee` contract, get the pilot customer to sign it, declare a paying commercial pilot." That is the wrong move and is exactly the kind of step that the canonical strategy and the 2026-04-25 critical review warn against. Three reasons.

First, Torres Vedras is the only `executed carrier` of the operating object (`executed-vs-designed.md §3`, `current-business-model.md §7.1`) and its working-version facts are: off-grid micro-farm, founder-built, founder-reported `client-funded CapEx`, verbal agreement, no signed written carrier, no recurring-payment trail, no supplier-contract bill-vs-bill baseline, founder-reported maintenance check after the 30 January storms. `regulatory-routing-and-power-tier-matrix.md §4` and `claim-register.md` row "Torres Vedras proves grid-connected UPAC / final-consumer baseline" both state explicitly that Torres Vedras **does not prove** the prospective Track B baseline. Extending it into Track B by signing a forward-looking recurring-service contract today contaminates the evidence rather than completing it.

Second, the desk research from `research/extra-perplexity/` (synthesised in `2026-04-25-critical-review-of-legal-research-files.md`) confirms that several of the contract clauses a forward-looking pilot template would normally include are exactly the clauses that need counsel sign-off first. Buy-back terms could re-classify the contract as consumer credit under DL 133/2009 and CCD2. Anything labelled `civil lease` or `subscription` instead of `fixed service fee` is a UTS terminology breach. A `minimum 20% savings guaranteed` clause runs ahead of the substantiation dossier required by the EU Empowering Consumers Directive (2024/825) and PT DL 57/2008. None of these can be safely written into a Torres Vedras-signed document before Tracks B.1, B.2, B.3 return counsel opinions.

Third, "the commercialisation part" of the pilot, *correctly read*, means converting Torres Vedras from `customer installation with incomplete commercial wrapper` into `external-grade evidence pack` per `open-gates.md` Priority 1 row 1. That is a memorialisation task, not a sales task. Once the evidence pack exists, the project can use Torres Vedras with counsel, investors and partners. The next paying customer should sign a separate, counsel-reviewed contract template, not a copy of Torres Vedras paperwork.

In short: the pilot customer should be asked to sign a **memorialisation pack**, not a future-commercial template. Its purpose is to make executed reality auditable and to preserve optionality, while keeping the door open to a properly structured commercial conversion later.

## 2. Recommended signature pack — four documents

The pack below is calibrated to do four things at once: lock down what already happened, allocate present rights cleanly, secure the data layer, and reserve future optionality without prejudicing the Track B legal package. It is designed to be drafted by Miguel (or by a Portuguese energy / consumer / civil-contract attorney) and signed by both parties; it is not a DIY template.

### 2.1 Document A — Pilot Memorialisation & Acknowledgement Agreement *(`acordo de memorização e reconhecimento do piloto`)*

This is the central document and the one the customer should be asked to sign first. It is a one-off, pilot-specific, retrospective agreement, not a commercial offer. It must contain, as a minimum, the following sections.

A short factual recital that names the parties, identifies the site, describes the installation, and acknowledges the chronology in plain language: who proposed what, when the equipment was installed, who paid for what (with reference to invoices and bank transfers), and that the installation has been operating off-grid since commissioning. The recital also names what the pilot is *not*: it is not a grid-connected UPAC, the customer has not registered as autoconsumidor with DGEG, no supplier-contract baseline exists, and no recurring monthly fee has been charged or collected.

A present-rights section that allocates, as of the signature date, four things: who currently holds title to the equipment, who has access to the site, who is responsible for maintenance, and who can de-install. The right answer for each comes from the actual Torres Vedras facts (working version: customer holds title because customer funded CapEx; site access for WeRa is permitted under a limited servidão / right of access for monitoring and maintenance; maintenance currently performed by founder; de-installation by mutual written agreement only). Whatever the actual answer is, it must be **stated**, not inferred.

A data-rights section that is tight and short: WeRa is permitted to collect, export, retain and use telemetry from the installation for the purposes of operational monitoring, fleet R&D and product development; the customer is acknowledged as the data subject for any household-energy data; processing is on the basis of contract performance and legitimate interest under the GDPR / Lei 58/2019; data is hosted intra-EU (Hetzner DE backup is acceptable, no Schrems II transfer-impact assessment is needed for this pilot); retention period is stated; the customer can revoke consent for non-operational uses with 30 days' notice. This section also references the separate Document B below.

An insurance and liability allocation section that sets out, even if minimally, who insures the equipment today, what the customer's homeowner / farm insurance covers and does not cover, what WeRa covers (likely nothing yet — and that fact must be stated honestly), and how force majeure is handled. If insurance is currently absent, the section should say so and commit to obtaining at least basic equipment-and-third-party insurance within a defined window.

A maintenance-and-incident section that adopts and forward-projects the existing log: the 30 January 2026 storm-check is recorded as the first formal entry, future inspections are scheduled (suggested cadence: biannual), the customer agrees to report any incident within 48 hours.

An optionality / future-commercial-conversion clause that is **explicitly non-binding**. Wording in the spirit of: *"The parties acknowledge that, separately and after appropriate legal advice, they may negotiate in good faith a forward-looking commercial arrangement covering some or all of the equipment installed at the site, including but not limited to a buy-back, a structured fixed service-fee arrangement, or a participation mechanism. Nothing in this agreement constitutes such arrangement. The parties expressly defer the choice of structure, label and price terms to a future agreement to be reviewed by Portuguese energy, consumer-finance and corporate counsel."* This single clause is what preserves the path to commercialisation without locking in terms now.

A clear scope-limitation clause stating that this agreement is a one-off pilot memorialisation, that nothing in it constitutes a representation by WeRa that it operates as `comercializador de energia eléctrica`, energy supplier, financial lessor, credit intermediary, or fund manager, and that the agreement does not extend to any other site or any other customer.

Counterparts, governing law (Portuguese), exclusive jurisdiction (the competent civil court of the customer's residence, per consumer-protection requirements), an integration clause, a severability clause, and signatures with date and place.

### 2.2 Document B — Data Processing & Telemetry Consent *(`consentimento RGPD para tratamento de dados de telemetria`)*

A short, GDPR-grade data-processing document that names WeRa as controller, identifies the categories of data collected (energy production, energy consumption, devices status, no special-category data, no biometric data, no household occupancy patterns beyond what telemetry strictly implies), the lawful basis (Art. 6(1)(b) and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), the storage location (local edge device first, intra-EU backup), retention (e.g. 5 years operational + 7 years for evidentiary purposes, to be confirmed), the subject's rights (access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection), the route to exercise them (a named contact at WeRa), the right to lodge a complaint with CNPD, and the customer's signature. This document is referenced by Document A but signed separately so it can be updated without re-opening the main agreement.

### 2.3 Document C — As-Built Inventory & Payment-Trail Acknowledgement *(`auto de constatação técnica e financeira`)*

A short document with two annexes. Annex 1 is a photographed, serial-numbered inventory of every component installed at the site, signed by both parties as a true record of what is on-site as of the signature date. Annex 2 is a payment-trail summary that lists, for each component (panels, inverter, batteries, edge device, mounting, cabling, labour), the source of funds, the date, the invoice number and the bank-transfer reference, with copies of invoices and SEPA transfer confirmations attached. The customer signs that the financial chronology is true. WeRa signs that the technical chronology is true. This document is the single most important piece of evidence-pack work and should be drafted by the team and reviewed by Miguel for adequacy as a counterparty document.

### 2.4 Document D — Site-Access and Servidão Memorandum *(`memorando de acesso ao local e servidão de uso`)*

A simple, time-bounded right-of-access instrument. It does not need to be a formal `servidão predial` registered with the conservatória — that would be premature. It is a contractual right-of-access for WeRa staff or contracted technicians for the purposes of monitoring, maintenance, telemetry verification and de-installation, at reasonable notice, for a defined period (e.g. five years renewable) and revocable by mutual written agreement. It includes liability-during-access language and an indemnity for damage caused by WeRa during access. This document protects WeRa's ability to physically visit the equipment without making any property-law claim that would otherwise need to go through a notary.

## 3. What we should explicitly NOT ask the pilot customer to sign

Each of the following clauses or instruments looks tempting because it sounds like "real commercialisation". Each of them, signed today at Torres Vedras, would damage the project's strategic position more than it would help. Honest list, with the reason.

A forward-looking 20- or 25-year recurring-service contract at a fixed monthly fee — premature, because the canonical service-fee package is a `selected working baseline for testing` per `starter-drr.md` and `claim-register.md`, not a counsel-clean structure, and because Torres Vedras as off-grid object cannot carry the grid-connected UPAC / final-consumer architecture the future template will need.

A buy-back agreement at a predetermined price (the path founder-reported as discussed during the pilot conversation) — premature, because Track B.2 desk research and CCD2 explicitly flag predetermined buy-back as a financial-lease / consumer-credit trigger, and because a signed buy-back commitment now will be cited later as evidence of consumer-credit substance even if the rest of the structure is service-flavoured.

A `civil lease (locação civil)` contract under CC Art. 1022, on the Otovo / EDEA model recommended by the quarantined `Optimal Legal Architecture` memo — premature and **terminologically off-baseline**, because UTS §3 explicitly rejects `lease fee`, `service fee`, `facility fee` as interchangeable labels and because the canonical baseline label is `fixed service fee`, not `civil lease`. The civil-lease path is a real alternative but its selection is exactly the decision that requires counsel and a `starter-drr.md` row, not a Torres Vedras signature.

A `minimum 20% savings guaranteed` clause or any savings-percentage commitment — premature, because the substantiation dossier required by Directive (EU) 2024/825 (Empowering Consumers / Green Claims, enforceable from September 2026) and by DL 57/2008 has not been built; Torres Vedras has no supplier-contract bill-vs-bill baseline against which any savings can be measured; and signing such a clause exposes WeRa to ASAE / DGC enforcement risk in a precedent-setting way.

A customer-to-shareholder conversion mechanism, a token grant, a CIC subscription right, or any participation-rights instrument — premature, because all of these sit on the Track B.3 corporate-securities-tax counsel track which is unanswered, and because triggering them at the single-pilot stage creates Prospectus Regulation, MiCA, CMVM and securities-offering questions that the project has explicitly demoted.

An assignment of the equipment from the customer to WeRa — premature, because the working-version facts say the customer paid for the equipment and therefore probably owns it; a transfer to WeRa today would either be a sale (with VAT, IRS and stamp-duty consequences not yet modelled) or a contribution (with corporate / shareholder-loan / quasi-equity consequences). If the team later concludes that asset-on-WeRa-balance-sheet is the right path, that transfer should happen under a separate, deliberate sale-or-contribution transaction reviewed by counsel.

A clause representing that the installation is registered with DGEG, has a comercializador licence, or is operating as a UPAC under DL 15/2022 — false today (off-grid, no MCP filed) and dangerous to put in writing. The recital in Document A should state the opposite: *no DGEG MCP has been filed; the installation is operating off-grid; the parties acknowledge this fact.*

## 4. Why this pack is the right "next step" rather than a smaller or larger one

Smaller (e.g. a one-page acknowledgement signed by the customer that WeRa installed equipment at the site) is not enough. It does not deliver the external-grade evidence pack that `open-gates.md` Priority 1 requires. It would not be sufficient for Miguel to assess the legal posture of the pilot. It would not help an investor doing due diligence. And it would not protect WeRa's ability to keep visiting the site or to use the telemetry.

Larger (e.g. a forward-looking 20-year service contract template signed by the pilot customer) is too much, for the reasons in Section 3 above. It also incurs the cost of a counsel-reviewed forward-template before counsel has delivered the answers that should shape the template.

The four-document pack in Section 2 is the smallest package that simultaneously: (a) makes Torres Vedras auditable as Track A executed evidence, (b) cleans up present rights so the team can operate the site without tension, (c) secures the data layer so the digital wedge is not foreclosed, and (d) preserves the optionality to convert this site into a properly structured commercial relationship later.

## 5. Sequencing — what happens after the customer signs

Step one, signed pack lands. Torres Vedras moves from `customer installation with incomplete commercial wrapper` to `auditable Track A pilot with executed memorialisation`. `claim-register.md` row `Torres Vedras already proves the full commercial baseline` can move from `partial / overstated if read too strongly` to `partial — auditable as Track A only`. The row `Torres Vedras proves grid-connected UPAC / final-consumer baseline` stays at `not proven / should not be claimed`.

Step two, the signed pack and the working-version pilot questionnaire are sent to Miguel together with the three pre-counsel memos B.1, B.2, B.3. Counsel can now respond to a real factual record, not a founder narrative. The desk research from `research/extra-perplexity/` accompanies the pack but is labelled `desk-grade input, not counsel opinion`.

Step three, counsel returns red / yellow / green per variant. The team revisits `starter-drr.md` and either confirms the existing baseline (`customer or project SPV title + WeRa as operator + fixed service fee`) or, if counsel says otherwise, opens the explicit `Solar-side ownership mode revisit (post-desk-research)` row recommended in the 2026-04-25 critical review.

Step four, the team drafts the **first repeatable commercial template**, separate from the Torres Vedras pack, and uses it for the next customer (the one currently in proposal stage) and for the Sintra-installer-channel opportunities. This template embodies whatever counsel confirms. Torres Vedras is referenced as Track A evidence. Future installations are referenced as Track B objects.

Step five, only after step four, the team and the Torres Vedras customer can come back to the optionality clause in Document A and negotiate, if they wish, a forward-looking commercial arrangement on the now-counsel-cleaned template. This is where the commercialisation of the pilot, in the strict sense, actually closes. Not before.

## 6. Drafting note for the team

This pack should be drafted by Miguel (or a comparable Portuguese energy / consumer / civil-contract attorney) on the basis of this recommendation, the Torres Vedras questionnaire and `SolarSeed.pilot.tv.md`. It should not be drafted from a generic online template. The cost of drafting four short documents of this type, with one round of revision, is small compared with the cost of a Torres Vedras signature on the wrong document. The signed pack is the artefact that turns the project's `executed carrier` from a founder anecdote into something a counsel, an investor and a partner can read.
