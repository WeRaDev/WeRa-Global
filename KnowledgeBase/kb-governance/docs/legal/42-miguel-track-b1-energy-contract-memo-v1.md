# Miguel Counsel Memo — Track B.1: Energy and Contract Package

> This memo is about the **future repeatable WeRa energy-service contract package**. It is not asking counsel to characterise what already happened in Torres Vedras. That is Track A.

## 1. Core Ask

Please help us validate and shape the Portuguese energy-law and customer-contract side of WeRa's selected working baseline:

> customer remains final consumer; WeRa acts as third-party solar operator / service-layer holder; solar asset title preferably sits with customer or project SPV; customer pays a fixed service fee; there is no disguised `€/kWh` electricity resale.

We need to know:

- whether this baseline is viable in Portugal;
- which contract type and contract package are safest;
- which words, fee mechanics and role descriptions to avoid;
- whether the model can avoid supplier / reseller classification;
- what `DGEG` / `ERSE`-facing wording would be safe after counsel review, if any authority contact is needed.

This memo is the energy-law route. It should not be used to answer consumer-credit licensing, credit-intermediation, fund/securities, tax or privacy questions except to flag them for the correct specialist track.

## 2. Entity Status Context

Please do not assume that a fully registered WeRa operating company already exists.

- WeRa Global is currently pre-registration; Portuguese registration is a Q2 2026 target.
- Current arrangements and pilot work are founder-level / pre-formation unless a document proves otherwise.
- No selected corporate structure is assumed for this memo; `PT CIC`, `NL STAK`, project SPV and ordinary PT operating company remain open or specialist-counsel hypotheses.
- Please answer which future contracting entity is needed for the first clean customer contract, without assuming STAK/CIC as given.

## 3. Why This Is Separate From Track A

Track A is retrospective and concerns Torres Vedras.

Track B.1 is prospective and concerns the future repeatable energy / customer-contract package.

Torres Vedras currently provides technical/operator evidence but does not prove:

- repeatable commercial baseline;
- recurring-payment behaviour;
- clean contract package;
- measured savings;
- finance-ready default / insurance / ownership stack;
- grid-connected UPAC / self-consumption compliance;
- customer-as-final-consumer treatment inside an ordinary supplier-contract relationship.

Please do not use Torres Vedras as proof that the prospective model is already executed.

## 4. Selected Baseline To Test

The selected baseline is:

- solar self-consumption installation at customer site;
- customer remains final consumer;
- WeRa is third-party operator / service-layer holder;
- installer partner performs installation and technical compliance;
- solar asset title preferably sits with customer or project SPV, not automatically on WeRa balance sheet;
- WeRa charges fixed service fee / neutral monthly payment;
- no retail electricity resale;
- no `€/kWh` customer billing;
- edge device is accessory telemetry infrastructure;
- optional digital services are not part of the legal identity of the first object.

This title posture is **selected for testing**, not legally confirmed clean. Counsel should compare customer title, project-SPV title and WeRa-title true lease side by side from the energy-law perspective, while flagging any consumer-credit or corporate/securities questions for Track B.2/B.3.

## 4A. Regulatory Routing and Power / Grid-Status Gate

Please route this memo as Portuguese energy / contract work only.

Before any authority-specific energy summary, please confirm:

- whether the relevant authority route is `DGEG`, `ERSE`, both, or neither at this stage;
- which power / grid-status bucket applies to the first repeatable installation: isolated/off-grid, grid-connected `<=700 W`, `>700 W` and `<=30 kW`, `>30 kW` and `<=1 MW`, multi-module, or collective / multi-IU;
- whether the orally discussed `21-25 kW` threshold has any formal legal relevance for WeRa's first customer contracts, or is only an installer-side memory that must not be operationalised;
- whether different customer segments (household, farm, SME, microbusiness) change the energy-law answer.

## 5. Explicit Non-Baseline Items

Please treat the following as outside this memo's baseline unless explicitly marked as a question:

- WeRa as electricity retailer / commercializadora;
- WeRa as installer of record;
- WeRa-owned asset true lease as default posture;
- customer-shareholder conversion;
- NL STAK -> PT CIC as mandatory starting structure;
- tokenised real-estate payment or priority buyout rights;
- universal `20%` savings guarantee;
- Torres Vedras as proof of repeatable commercial execution.

## 6. Questions For Counsel

### 6.1 Portuguese Contract Type

1. Under Portuguese Código Civil, which contractual type is the safest legal frame for the selected baseline: `prestação de serviços`, `locação`, `contrato misto/atípico`, or another form?
2. What are the consequences of each choice for tax, liability, enforcement and consumer-protection treatment?
3. Should the customer-facing contract avoid the word `lease` unless true `locação` is intentionally selected?
4. Is `fixed service fee` a safe commercial label, or should another Portuguese label be used?
5. Can the fee be calculated using installation cost, contract duration, maintenance, target return and customer-bill baseline without becoming disguised `€/kWh` electricity resale?

### 6.1A Alternative Characterisation — `Hosting Frame`

A parallel characterisation has been raised internally and we want a written view from you before treating §6.1 as final:

> WeRa could be described as a `hosting provider` for the customer's electrical equipment (solar installation, inverter, storage, edge / telemetry layer), by analogy to infrastructure / colocation hosting in the IT sector. The customer remains owner and final consumer of the equipment; WeRa provides a hosting-level service wrapper covering installation coordination, monitoring, maintenance, operational continuity and billing.

Questions:

5A. Is this characterisation jurisdictionally real in Portugal — i.e., does Portuguese law recognise a `hosting / outsourcing of physical equipment at the customer's site` form (or a close functional equivalent: `contrato de prestação de serviços de gestão e operação de equipamento`, `contrato de outsourcing`, `contrato atípico`) that could legitimately frame the same economic content?

5B. If yes:
   - which Civil Code type is closest;
   - does this route move us into a cleaner contractual frame than the `service-fee + operator around UPAC` route, or does it create new consumer-services / outsourcing / data-processing duties that are heavier;
   - does it change the answer on title (customer / SPV / WeRa), VAT, consumer-protection, or default / de-installation rights;
   - does it interact with consumer-credit classification differently than the UPAC route (this is for Track B.2 to confirm, but please flag the direction).

5C. If no — why specifically the hosting frame fails:
   - anti-circumvention rules in PT energy law that force UPAC / `autoconsumo` classification regardless of label;
   - mandatory operator / supplier classification triggered by the activity itself;
   - consumer-protection rules that override service / hosting framing for residential customers;
   - any other doctrine that closes this path.

5D. Independent of (5A–5C), please confirm or correct the routing implication:
   - does a hosting characterisation, if real, shift any part of the regulator routing in `regulatory-routing-and-power-tier-matrix.md` §1 (e.g. away from `DGEG` / `ERSE` for the contract-defining frame, leaving them only for technical / safety compliance);
   - or does the routing matrix in §1 hold regardless of which contractual frame is chosen.

This is a parallel arbitrage check, not a baseline replacement. The selected baseline remains `service-fee + operator`. We are asking you to confirm the hosting frame is either viable, neutral or closed before we operationalise §6.1.

### 6.2 UPAC / Self-Consumption / Final Consumer

6. Can this model be structured so the customer remains final consumer / `autoconsumidor` in Portugal?
7. Can WeRa act as third-party operator / service-layer holder around a UPAC/self-consumption installation?
8. What must be true for this not to become retail electricity supply?
9. Who should handle registration / notification / technical compliance?
10. Who handles excess generation / `excedentes`?
11. Can WeRa receive or manage any economic benefit from excess generation, and if so under what structure?

### 6.3 Asset Title From Energy-Law Perspective

12. Which asset-title posture is safest for first contracts from energy-law perspective, assuming none is yet confirmed clean:
   - customer title;
   - project SPV title;
   - WeRa title as true lease / heavier alternative?
13. If customer title is used, how does WeRa retain operating, monitoring, access and maintenance rights?
14. If project SPV title is used, who contracts with customer and who is recognised in the energy-law documents?
15. If WeRa title is used, does energy-law treatment become easier, harder, or simply different?
16. What end-of-term options are cleanest: extension, transfer, purchase option, de-installation, or residual-value treatment?

### 6.3A Power / Grid-Status Tiers

16A. Please map the first contracts across the power/grid-status tiers in `regulatory-routing-and-power-tier-matrix.md`.
16B. Does an isolated/off-grid installation follow a materially different legal route from a grid-connected UPAC installation?
16C. Can multiple modules at the same site be assessed separately, or will authorities aggregate them for threshold purposes?
16D. What documents, notifications, certifications or grid steps are required for `>700 W` and `<=30 kW` versus `>30 kW` and `<=1 MW`?

### 6.4 Installer Partner

17. What role must the installer partner legally play?
18. Which installation / connection obligations must remain with the installer?
19. What warranties, insurance and compliance undertakings should installer provide to WeRa and/or customer?
20. Can WeRa coordinate installation without being treated as installer of record?

### 6.5 Default, De-Installation and Insurance

21. What happens if the customer stops paying under the service contract?
22. What notice and cure periods are required?
23. Can WeRa suspend service without affecting electricity-supply rights?
24. Can WeRa access the site and de-install equipment?
25. Who pays for de-installation?
26. What restrictions apply for households / consumers / microbusinesses?
27. What insurance must exist for fire, roof damage, third-party damage, theft, vandalism, storm, hail and force majeure?
28. Who should be named insured or beneficiary?

### 6.6 Supply Relationship Management

Founder hypothesis, not baseline:

> WeRa may simplify customer experience by managing or taking over the residual grid-supply relationship.

Questions:

29. Can WeRa manage the supplier relationship as authorised agent without becoming supplier?
30. Can WeRa be the contract holder for residual electricity while the customer remains final consumer?
31. Would this create electricity resale, aggregation, supply or consumer-protection obligations?
32. What should be the safest baseline: customer retains supply contract, WeRa only advises / coordinates?

### 6.7 Data / Telemetry

33. What data-protection role does WeRa have for telemetry and customer energy data?
34. What consent / notice is required?
35. Can WeRa use telemetry and bills to calculate discounts and savings?
36. What retention and access rules should apply?

### 6.8 Portugal / Spain Comparison

37. Can you opine, or refer us to an ES colleague, on whether the same contract stack would be legally cleaner in Spain?
38. We are not asking to relocate the model now; we only need to understand whether PT and ES differ materially for this selected operating object.

## 7. Requested Output From Counsel

Please provide:

1. A red/yellow/green assessment of the selected Portuguese energy-service baseline.
2. The recommended Portuguese contract type and customer-facing label.
3. A recommended contract package and role map.
4. A preferred asset-title posture for first contracts from energy-law perspective.
5. A list of words / structures to avoid.
6. A default / de-installation / insurance checklist.
7. A `DGEG` / `ERSE`-safe short description of the model, if counsel thinks such external wording is needed.
8. A power/grid-status matrix confirmation for first contracts.
9. A list of questions that require consumer-credit or corporate/securities/tax specialist counsel.
10. If Track A and Track B answers imply incompatible legal forms for the Torres Vedras object, please flag this explicitly and propose a compatible memorialisation path.
