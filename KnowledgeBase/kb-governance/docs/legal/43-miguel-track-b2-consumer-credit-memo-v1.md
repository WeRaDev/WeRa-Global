# Miguel Counsel Memo — Track B.2: Consumer Credit and Financial Intermediation

> This memo is about **financial-regulatory classification** of the future repeatable WeRa model. It is not an energy-law memo and not a Torres Vedras retrospective memo.

## 1. Core Ask

Please help us determine whether WeRa's prospective model could trigger consumer-credit, credit-intermediation, financial-lease, lending, disclosure or related licensing obligations.

The selected commercial baseline is:

> customer remains final consumer; WeRa acts as third-party solar operator / service-layer holder; solar asset title preferably sits with customer or project SPV; customer pays a fixed service fee; no disguised `€/kWh` electricity resale.

We want to keep the first baseline as far as possible from any structure that requires consumer-credit licensing or equivalent financial-regulatory authorisation.

This memo is the financial-regulatory route. If counsel thinks an authority question is needed, the likely route is consumer-finance counsel first and then `Banco de Portugal`, not `DGEG` / `ERSE` and not the corporate/securities route.

## 2. Entity Status Context

Please do not assume that a fully registered WeRa operating company already exists.

- WeRa Global is currently pre-registration; Portuguese registration is a Q2 2026 target.
- Current arrangements and pilot work are founder-level / pre-formation unless a document proves otherwise.
- No selected corporate structure is assumed for this memo; `PT CIC`, `NL STAK`, project SPV and ordinary PT operating company remain open or specialist-counsel hypotheses.
- Please state whether a future entity type changes the consumer-credit / intermediation answer.

## 3. Structures To Test

Please test the following variants separately:

| Variant | Current status | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Customer-title asset + WeRa operator / fixed service fee | `selected working baseline for testing / not yet clean` | May be cleanest operationally but can look like customer-financed equipment if payment logic is wrong. |
| Project-SPV-title asset + WeRa operator | `live scaling alternative` | May support asset finance but may add fund / securities / lending questions. |
| WeRa-title asset + customer true lease / service payment | `live but heavier alternative` | May be clearer as asset lease but may raise stronger consumer-credit / repossession issues. |
| Client-funded CapEX + future WeRa buy-back path | `pilot-style path / not selected baseline` | Needs separate classification before reuse. |
| Third-party bank or lender financing arranged by WeRa | `live financing route` | May make WeRa credit intermediary. |

## 3A. Routing Rules For This Memo

Please test whether the selected customer/project-SPV title posture makes consumer-credit risk better or worse than a true WeRa-owned lease.

Do not treat an energy-law answer as sufficient for this track:

- `final consumer` / UPAC viability does not settle consumer-credit classification;
- oral installer-side thresholds such as `21-25 kW` do not settle finance-law classification;
- installer-network or Frame IV referrals may create point-of-sale finance / credit-intermediation risk even if WeRa is not the lender;
- project-SPV title may move the issue into fund, securities, tax or collateral territory and should be flagged for Track B.3.

## 4. Questions For Counsel

### 4.1 Core Consumer-Credit Classification

1. Does a long-term recurring payment for installation access, operation and service risk being characterised as consumer credit?
2. Does this change if the customer is a household, sole trader, microbusiness, SME or farm?
3. Does a low or zero upfront payment increase consumer-credit risk?
4. Does long tenor, such as `240-360` months, increase consumer-credit or financial-lease risk?
5. Does a purchase option, transfer option, residual-value formula or end-of-term ownership transfer change the classification?
6. Does the legal answer differ between `fixed service fee`, `lease fee`, `monthly payment`, `subscription`, `service charge` or another label?

### 4.2 Asset Title Variants

7. If the customer holds asset title, can WeRa still charge a service fee without being seen as financing the customer's equipment acquisition?
8. If a project SPV holds title, does the customer payment become lease-like, credit-like, service-like or another form?
9. If WeRa holds title, does that reduce or increase consumer-credit risk?
10. Which title posture is safest if the aim is to avoid consumer-credit licensing?

### 4.3 Credit Intermediation / Financing Arrangement

11. Is WeRa arranging credit if it organises third-party CAPEX financing for the customer or project SPV?
12. Can WeRa introduce the customer to a lender without being a credit intermediary?
13. Can WeRa finance an SPV through equity or shareholder loan without becoming a consumer lender?
14. If the installer partner introduces customers to WeRa's financed/service structure, can that create point-of-sale finance or credit-intermediation issues?
15. Are licences, registrations, disclosures, APR calculations or total-cost-of-credit disclosures required?
16. Are there safe harbours or structuring principles for energy-service companies in this context?

### 4.4 Pilot-Style Buy-Back Path

17. If a pilot-style client-funded CapEX + future WeRa buy-back path is ever reused, does the buy-back step trigger consumer-credit / lease / securities qualification?
18. Does a future buy-back promise create present credit, reimbursement or investment-product implications?
19. How should any future buy-back option be worded if counsel allows it at all?
20. Should the first baseline exclude buy-back language entirely until financial-regulatory counsel signs off?

### 4.5 Default and Enforcement

21. If the customer defaults, what actions are allowed before the structure looks like repossession of financed goods?
22. Can WeRa suspend service?
23. Can WeRa de-install equipment?
24. Does de-installation differ if title is customer-held, SPV-held or WeRa-held?
25. What consumer-protection rules apply to arrears, notices, cure periods and collections?

### 4.6 Savings Claims, Discount Policy and Add-Ons

26. Can WeRa state a customer-specific projected discount against prior bills?
27. Can WeRa guarantee a discount if the customer provides 12 months of bills and device/load inventory?
28. Does a `20%` or higher discount promise create consumer-credit, unfair-commercial-practice or misleading-advertising risk?
29. Can discount tiers be tied to data availability?
30. Do bundled add-ons such as batteries, appliances, gardening, landscaping or pool maintenance create additional consumer-credit or instalment-sale risk?

## 5. Requested Output From Counsel

Please provide:

1. A red/yellow/green assessment of each structure in section 3.
2. The safest structure if WeRa wants to avoid consumer-credit / credit-intermediation licensing.
3. A list of contract labels and fee mechanics to avoid.
4. Required disclosures, registrations or licences if any.
5. Segment-specific differences for households, farms, SMEs and microbusinesses.
6. A view on whether pilot-style client-funded CapEX + future buy-back can ever be reused.
7. A view on whether any `Banco de Portugal` route is required before first contracts.
8. A statement that power thresholds such as `21-25 kW` do or do not matter for this classification.
9. A list of questions that require energy-law or corporate/securities/tax specialist counsel.
10. If Track A and Track B answers imply incompatible legal forms for the Torres Vedras object, please flag this explicitly and propose a compatible memorialisation path.
