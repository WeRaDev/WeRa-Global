## 1. Overview and Strategic Purpose

Sunified Group BV is a Netherlands-based deep-tech company that manufacturers a micro-sensor called **UNITY** directly into solar panels. The sensor creates a per-panel immutable data layer — effectively a trusted execution environment at the hardware level — that enables verified energy data, cybersecurity, Web3 connectivity, and quantum-resistant encryption across distributed solar assets.

**WeRa's interest in Sunified is as a supplier partner for the SolarSeed product**. Sunified is not a solar developer or cloud company; it is a sensor/data infrastructure provider whose UNITY technology would upgrade SolarSeed panels from their current form (AIKO/YH panels) into cryptographically authenticated, quantum-resistant energy nodes.

## 3. UNITY Sensor — Technical Overview

### What Is UNITY?

UNITY is a **low-cost, deep-tech micro-sensor manufactured directly into solar panels** during the fabrication process. It is not a clamp-on or plug-in addition — it is embedded at the panel level, creating an inseparable bond between the physical panel and its digital identity.

This means:
- Each panel has a **permanent, tamper-resistant digital identity from birth**
- Data produced by the panel cannot be separated from the physical asset
- The sensor creates a **Layer-0 immutable data record** — below the application layer, below the OS layer, at the hardware level

## 7. Quantum-Resistance Thesis

### The 2028 Cryptographic Threat Horizon

The global cryptographic infrastructure is approaching a critical inflection point. Current widely-deployed public-key cryptography (RSA, ECC) is based on mathematical problems (integer factorization, discrete logarithm) that quantum computers are expected to break at scale. Estimates for cryptographically-relevant quantum computers converge around **2028–2033**.

> "While other systems will fall in 2028, our system will be resistant."
> — Referenced in WeRa strategic context, relating to the post-quantum cryptography timeline

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) finalized its first post-quantum cryptography (PQC) standards in 2024 (FIPS 203, 204, 205 — based on CRYSTALS-Kyber and CRYSTALS-Dilithium lattice algorithms). Organizations that have not transitioned to PQC-compliant infrastructure before quantum computers reach cryptographic relevance face retroactive decryption of previously encrypted data (the "harvest now, decrypt later" attack vector).

### How UNITY Creates Quantum Resistance

Sunified's UNITY sensor, designed by Leon Vandenberg (35+ years cryptography background, including sovereign military-grade systems for Australian Defence and US Navy), is architected to support **post-quantum cryptographic primitives at the hardware level**.

The mechanism operates as follows:

```
Physical Panel (Hardware)
    └── UNITY Sensor (embedded, tamper-resistant)
            ├── Root of Trust (hardware-level key generation)
            ├── Trusted Execution Environment (TEE)
            │       └── Encrypts panel data before it leaves the sensor
            ├── PQC-compatible key infrastructure
            └── Immutable data layer (Layer-0)
                    └── Data anchored to blockchain → verifiable provenance
```

**Each UNITY-enabled panel becomes a TEE node**. When thousands of SolarSeed installations each incorporate UNITY panels, the aggregate network becomes a **distributed quantum-resistant encryption fabric** — a virtual digital energy station where:

1. Each node holds cryptographic keys at hardware level (not software, not cloud)
2. Keys are generated from quantum-resistant algorithms (not RSA/ECC)
3. Data produced by the solar asset is encrypted before transmission, eliminating man-in-the-middle attack surfaces
4. The distributed topology eliminates single points of cryptographic failure

### Why Distributed Matters

Centralized cryptographic systems have a single key management infrastructure that, if compromised, exposes all encrypted data. A network of thousands of UNITY-enabled SolarSeed nodes creates:

- **No single key store** — each panel has its own root of trust
- **No single failure point** — compromise of one node does not cascade
- **Byzantine Fault Tolerance by physics** — geographically distributed, physically separated nodes
- **Self-sovereign key custody** — the solar citizen controls the cryptographic identity of their installation

This architecture aligns precisely with WeRa's broader sovereignty thesis: energy sovereignty, data sovereignty, and now **cryptographic sovereignty**.

### Founder Credibility: NavyCash as Proof of Concept

Leon Vandenberg's US Navy NavyCash system (1999) is directly relevant here. NavyCash was a closed-loop monetary system deployed on US Navy ships — isolated networks, adversarial environments, high-stakes cryptographic integrity requirements. The design constraints of NavyCash (tamper-resistance, offline capability, sovereign key custody, no reliance on external PKI) mirror exactly the constraints of a UNITY-enabled solar network. This is not theoretical cryptography — it is battle-tested sovereign infrastructure design applied to renewable energy.

## 9. Panel Hardware Upgrade Path

Current SolarSeed panel configurations:

| Profile | Current Panel | UNITY-Enabled Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **Base (M-Profile)** | 6× YH SUNPRO SP505-132M10 (505W, 3.03 kWp) | 6× UNITY-embedded equivalent at similar wattage |
| **Average (L-Profile)** | 16× AIKO-A-MAH60Db Neostar 500W (8 kWp) | 16× UNITY-embedded equivalent (Astronergy partner for fabrication integration) |

**Upgrade pathway**: Sunified's R&D partnership is with **Astronergy (China)**, one of the world's leading panel manufacturers. AIKO and Astronergy are separate Chinese manufacturers, so the integration path requires either:
1. Sunified expanding its fabrication partnerships to include AIKO, or
2. WeRa transitioning future panel procurement to Astronergy-manufactured UNITY-enabled panels

This is a procurement and supply chain decision — not a product architecture change. The energy performance specs (wattage, efficiency) would be comparable; the addition of the UNITY sensor is the upgrade.
