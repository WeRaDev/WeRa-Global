# Automation Center Unfair Advantage

## Purpose
Document Automation Center's validated differentiation.

## Scope
- In scope: the discovery-and-ROI methodology as a moat, distinct from the underlying no-code/LLM tooling.
- Out of scope: Human Center's Shrinking AI differentiation (`10-unfair-advantage.md`).

## Current State
**The moat is the methodology, not the tools.** n8n, Make, Zapier, and LLM APIs are commodity building blocks available to any automation agency (the pricing research names at least seven comparable European/US agencies using the same stack). Automation Center's differentiation is the rigor of its discovery-and-ROI process, which the research explicitly designed to avoid the failure modes typical of this market:

1. **Screening vs. investment-grade ROI, kept explicitly separate.** Most competing agencies (implicitly, per the pricing benchmarks) sell on a quoted price without a documented, evidence-graded discovery process. Automation Center never promises a "guaranteed ROI" from a sales conversation; it produces a graded screening decision (Stop/Measure/Prototype/Pilot) and defers investment-grade ROI to matched pre/post pilot measurement.
2. **Evidence grading (A-E) with explicit provenance.** Every number behind a proposal is traceable to a grade (system record, sample, reconciled estimate, single recollection, or team assumption) rather than presented as an undifferentiated fact.
3. **Correct benefit classification.** Time saved is treated as capacity, not cash, until a named owner confirms how it converts to cash-releasing savings, cost avoidance, or incremental margin — avoiding the common mistake of inflating ROI by counting all released time at a billing rate.
4. **Knowledge-owner routing.** Each interview respondent (adviser, operations, compliance/data owner, optional finance owner) is asked only what they can reliably know, rather than a single generic "would AI help you" conversation.
5. **Compliance-aware from the first conversation.** The compliance/data-owner route is a mandatory feasibility gate for Financial Automation's regulated or financial-advisory clients (see `../../../kb-value-proposition/docs/products/04-financial-automation.md`), addressing a risk that generic automation agencies do not appear to manage explicitly.
6. **Positioning: "we automate deliverables, not tasks."** Per the founder's working note behind the MVP build, the unit of analysis is the deliverable — a concrete, owned, verifiable artifact (a document, report, reconciliation, decision, sale, or similar) with a recipient, a quality standard, a completion condition, and an observable business outcome. This is deliberately sharper than "we automate boring tasks": a task is vague and open-ended, a deliverable has an owner and an outcome, which is what makes the ROI screening methodology (above) attachable to it in the first place. The target is reducing manual human time on a deliverable by roughly 60-80% while the human retains verification, labeling, approval, and final accountability — not eliminating the role.

## Decisions / Rules
- Do not compete primarily on tooling sophistication (n8n vs. Make vs. Zapier); compete on the credibility and rigor of the discovery-and-ROI process.
- Always show the evidence grade behind any ROI number presented to a prospect or client.
- Do not let sales pressure convert a screening estimate into an implied investment-grade promise.
- Frame every engagement around a named deliverable, not a generic "AI automation" pitch; if a prospect cannot name a specific deliverable and its recipient, the opportunity is not yet qualified (consistent with the discovery methodology's requirement for a bounded recurring unit).
