# Problem

## Purpose
Define the core problem Shrinking AI's target customers face, as the basis for its scoping.

## Scope
- In scope: the "AI adoption fails without human facilitation" problem for SME/consultant/freelancer buyers; the emotional/trust barrier preventing buyers from seeking help.
- Out of scope: enterprise-scale AI transformation programs; Empowering Human, Human Center's other (hypothesis-stage) service.

## Current State
**Core framing (cross-validated, 3+ independent sources): AI adoption failure is a facilitation/expertise gap, not a technology gap** — "a learning gap, not a tech gap." Key validated statistics:
- 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots fail to reach production / deliver P&L impact (MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide", corroborated by Forbes, Fortune, Unframe, AI in Time).
- ~5% of custom enterprise AI tools ever reach production (MIT NANDA).
- **Vendor/partner-led AI implementations succeed at 67% vs. 33% for internal/DIY builds** — the single strongest evidentiary pillar for Shrinking AI's value proposition ("guided implementation beats DIY").
- EU AI adoption gap: 55% of large enterprises vs. 17% of small enterprises (10-49 employees) — a 38-point gap (Omago SME data brief).
- 50-71% of non-adopting businesses cite "lack of expertise" as their primary barrier.
- 88% of agentic AI pilots never reach production; 61% of users report AI fell short of ROI expectations; 56% of the workforce has received no training on the AI tools they are expected to use.
- 40%+ of agentic AI projects are predicted to be cancelled by 2027 (Gartner); AI project failure rate is 80%+ vs. roughly 2x lower for non-AI IT projects (RAND).

**Verbatim buyer pain language** (LinkedIn desk research, tagged by emotional tone):
- "We're paying for Copilot, ChatGPT, and two other tools. Nobody on the team actually knows how to use them properly." (fatigue/frustration)
- "I feel like I'm the problem — like I should know how to use this better, but nobody taught me what 'better' actually looks like." (embarrassment/sunk-cost)
- "I lost two hours of my day" (on AI tools creating more admin overhead than they saved). (fatigue/frustration)
- "AI isn't failing because employees resist it... they don't trust it." (defensiveness/distrust)

**The dominant emotion is embarrassment mixed with sunk-cost frustration, not anger.** Buyers feel exposed for not already understanding AI. This is a hard design/messaging constraint, not just a tone preference.

A broader, complementary research thread (from an earlier, more academic pass) identifies a related mechanism at the model-behavior level: emotionally/rhetorically "dirty" human input (vague, emotionally-loaded, poorly-specified prompts) measurably increases AI output variance and the downstream verification/correction burden, which is one contributor to why individual-level AI productivity gains fail to aggregate into firm-level returns (the "aggregation paradox").

## Decisions / Rules
- Copy, positioning, and the sales conversation must never imply the buyer or their team is "behind," "resistant," or "doing it wrong" — any framing that feels like a sales pitch or an implicit "you're doing it wrong" triggers defensive disengagement.
- Lead with the "guided implementation beats DIY" statistic (67% vs. 33%, or the 95%-fail framing) rather than a generic "AI can help your business" claim; buyers must self-identify as already having tried and failed.
- Frame the problem narrowly per client (a specific bottleneck), not as generic "AI transformation," per the Design Thinking / Theory of Constraints research (see `../../../kb-solution/docs/strategy/02-solution.md`).
