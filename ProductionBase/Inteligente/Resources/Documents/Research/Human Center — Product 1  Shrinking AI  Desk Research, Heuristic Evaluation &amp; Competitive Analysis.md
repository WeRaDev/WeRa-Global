# Human Center — Product 1 "Shrinking AI" (B2B Consultancy): Desk Research, Heuristic Evaluation & Competitive Analysis for Website Design

## Executive Synthesis

Human Center's Product 1, "Shrinking AI," addresses a validated and documented market failure: organisations are spending on AI tools without corresponding ROI, and the root cause is not the technology but the quality of human input into it — a problem LinkedIn evidence independently confirms as "a learning gap, not a tech gap". Desk research shows 88% of agentic AI pilots never reach production, 95% of enterprise AI pilots fail to deliver expected value, 61% of users report AI has fallen short of ROI expectations, and 56% of the workforce has received no training on the AI tools they are expected to use. No identified competitor addresses the emotional friction layer that sits "below the strategy level and above the tool level" — this is Shrinking AI's structural white space.[^1]

The primary design goal for the Human Center website — booking a 30-minute call — must be engineered against two conflicting forces uncovered in this research: enterprise/B2B buyers scrutinise consulting sites for restraint, evidence, and process depth before they trust a CTA, while the specific ICP validated for Shrinking AI (AI-frustrated consultants and micro-SME owners) responds best to low-pressure, emotionally intelligent, non-corporate framing that signals "zero judgment". The website must therefore borrow McKinsey/Accenture-grade credibility architecture while rejecting their heaviest usability flaw: burying the commercial path.[^2][^3]

## Product and Market Grounding

Human Center is a consultancy project of Inteligente Razão — Unipessoal LDA, operating a human-facilitated service that converts "AI frustration" into bounded, actionable workflows. Shrinking AI's current sellable object is a session flow — a free 15-minute empathy call followed by a paid structured session — producing a concrete AI-ready problem statement and instruction set, with an optional path into retainer or implementation support. Geography is Western Europe and the Nordics, targeting companies with budgets for AI and cultures of supporting employees with organisational tools, and the core value message is "we will help you get better results for the same money and keep your people happy".[^3]

Scoring across four validated B2B segments places the AI-Frustrated Consultant/Freelancer (Segment A4) as the primary ICP (score 91/100) and the Micro-SME Owner (Segment A1) as secondary (86/100), ahead of Ops Leads in small organisations (84) and Independent Professionals (79). This matters directly for website design: copy, proof, and CTA framing should be built primarily around a peer-to-peer professional register (consultant speaking to consultant) rather than a generic SME sales register.[^4]

| Segment | Pain intensity (25%) | Readiness to act (20%) | Willingness to pay (20%) | Channel efficiency (15%) | Upgrade potential (10%) | Case-study quality (10%) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A4 – AI-frustrated consultant/freelancer | 22 | 19 | 18 | 14 | 9 | 9 | **91**[^4] |
| A1 – Micro-SME owner | 23 | 18 | 16 | 13 | 8 | 8 | **86**[^4] |
| A2 – Ops lead, small org | 21 | 16 | 17 | 12 | 9 | 9 | **84**[^4] |
| A3 – Independent professional | 20 | 17 | 15 | 13 | 7 | 7 | **79**[^4] |

## Verbatim Pain Language and the Trust Barrier

LinkedIn desk research surfaced consistent, verbatim pain language from SME leadership: "We're paying for Copilot, ChatGPT, and two other tools. Nobody on the team actually knows how to use them properly," and "I feel like I'm the problem — like I should know how to use this better, but nobody taught me what 'better' actually looks like". The dominant emotion identified is not anger but embarrassment mixed with sunk-cost frustration — buyers feel exposed for not already understanding AI, which is a critical design constraint: any conversion path, tone, or CTA that feels like a sales pitch or an implicit "you're doing it wrong" will trigger defensive disengagement.[^3]

This directly informs the free 15-minute empathy session as the highest-leverage conversion mechanism identified in the research: it removes the primary trust barrier (fear of being sold to) before any paid ask is made. The validated "Role, Context, Goal, Format, Constraints" structured-input framework mirrors the deliverable Human Center already promises, which means the website should visibly teach a fragment of this framework as a trust-building preview rather than gating all value behind a call.[^3]

## Competitive Landscape

Desk research maps five competitor categories relevant to Shrinking AI, none of which directly replicates the emotionally-aware, session-based, two-hour-turnaround facilitation model:[^3]

| Category | Example type | What they offer | Gap vs. Shrinking AI |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI change management consultants | Enterprise transformation firms | Strategy roadmaps, org-wide rollouts | Target enterprise, not SME; slow, expensive, strategy-first |
| Prompt engineering trainers | Course/certificate providers | Self-service courses, prompt templates | No facilitation, no emotional layer, generic outputs |
| AI productivity coaches | Individual coaching | 1:1 coaching for personal AI use | Targets individuals, not organisations |
| Generic IT/AI implementation consultants | Freelance/agency IT consultants | Tool setup, workflow configuration | Exactly what SMEs already tried and found ineffective — sets up tools but doesn't fix interaction quality [^3] |
| Boutique "book a 30-min AI call" agencies | AI Jungle, Benian Technologies, The Automators | Free discovery call → scoped automation project | Positioned around automation/ROI discovery, not emotional-input diagnostics; still useful structural benchmark for booking-page mechanics [^5][^6][^7] |

The boutique AI-agency booking pages reviewed (AI Jungle's "Book a 30-Min AI Workforce Call," Benian's "Book Your AI Discovery Call," The Automators' "free 30-minute discovery call") show a converging pattern for lightweight B2B scheduling: a single embedded scheduler, one clear CTA, minimal form friction, and a plain-language outcome promise before the click. This is the tactical benchmark for Human Center's own booking mechanism, distinct from the strategic/credibility benchmark set by McKinsey-tier firms.[^5][^6][^7]

## Heuristic Evaluation of Tier-1 Consulting Homepages (McKinsey, Deloitte, BCG, Slalom)

A March 2026 structured homepage audit scored four major professional-services firms on presentation, organisation/messaging, conversion, and digital currency, each on a 1–4 scale (total /20). The findings are directly transferable to Human Center's design brief because they expose exactly which "prestige" patterns fail even world-class brands.[^2]

| Firm | Score /20 | Strength | Usability Flaw |
|---|---|---|---|
| McKinsey | 15 | Live "Ask McKinsey" AI chatbot; customer-outcome headline ("What's your next brilliant move?") | Capabilities buried below podcasts/video carousel; commercial path requires extra effort [^2] |
| Deloitte | 14 | Highest production/visual polish; motion-rich hero | Five-plus competing CTAs with no hierarchy; "Submit RFP" buried in footer despite being critical for buyers [^2] |
| BCG | 15 | Buyer-centric "How can we assist you today?" self-select dropdowns; considered, permanent headline | Static, flat, no above-the-fold CTA at all; Contact Us lives only in footer [^2] |
| Slalom | 17 (highest) | "Speak with us" CTA above the fold; named client logos, testimonials, AI metrics visible without a click; case studies in top-level nav | AI shown only via metrics/case studies, not as a live interactive feature [^2] |

Cross-firm findings with direct design implications for Human Center:

- **Prestige is not a conversion strategy.** Three of four major firms bury or fragment their commercial path, trusting reputation to do the selling — a luxury Human Center, as an unproven early-stage brand, cannot afford.[^2]
- **The smallest firm evaluated (Slalom) has the clearest buyer journey**, proving that conversion clarity is a design discipline, not a budget outcome.[^2]
- **Talking about AI vs. demonstrating it is the emerging differentiator** — only McKinsey shows a live AI interaction on its homepage; everyone else treats AI as a topic. Human Center has a distinct opportunity here: the free empathy session itself could be framed and even partially previewed as an interactive "diagnostic" experience embedded in the site.[^2]
- **Positioning consistency matters.** Deloitte's rotating hero (no permanent headline) was flagged as a structural weakness versus McKinsey, BCG, and Slalom's fixed, intentional headlines.[^2]

## Heuristic Evaluation Against Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics (Applied to Shrinking AI's Draft Funnel)

Using the standard heuristic evaluation framework for competitive UX analysis, the current Human Center concept (as described in project documentation, pre-website) presents the following heuristic risks and opportunities:[^8][^9]

| Heuristic | Current risk | Design opportunity for Shrinking AI site |
|---|---|---|
| Visibility of system status | No stated conversion funnel data or session-flow visibility yet on any live surface [^10] | Show a simple 3-step visual (Book free call → Structured session → Deliverable in 2 hours) so visitors always know where they are in the journey |
| Match between system and real world | Verbatim SME pain language exists in research but has not yet been tested on-site copy [^3] | Mirror validated phrases ("wasted time," "not getting what I asked for") instead of technical jargon ("prompt engineering," "LLM alignment") per messaging guardrails [^3] |
| User control and freedom | Single funnel path only (call booking); no lower-commitment or self-serve option surfaced | Offer a soft entry point (e.g., a short diagnostic quiz or downloadable "Role-Context-Goal-Format-Constraints" checklist) alongside the primary call CTA |
| Consistency and standards | Two very different product lines (B2B consultancy and B2C career training) risk brand and message collision under one domain [^3] | Strict page-level separation; Shrinking AI needs its own distinct navigation, tone, and visual system separate from Career Development |
| Error prevention | No stated pre-call qualification step; risk of unqualified leads booking calls and wasting founder bandwidth (a named operational risk) [^3] | Add a short pre-booking form (2–3 questions) to qualify fit and set expectations, reducing no-shows and mismatched calls |
| Recognition rather than recall | Case study and proof assets are explicitly listed as "missing" (10+ completed sessions, 3 case write-ups, consent evidence pack) [^3] | Until proof exists, substitute with concrete process visuals, the "2-hour deliverable" promise, and transparent "what you get" mockups rather than vague claims |
| Flexibility and efficiency of use | Enterprise buyers expect a downloadable capability statement / PDF for internal forwarding [^9] | Add a one-page PDF summary of the Shrinking AI offer for referral/consultant-led sales motion (A4 ICP) |
| Aesthetic and minimalist design | No current visual system on file | Adopt the enterprise-consulting visual pattern validated in research: restrained 1–2 colour palette, strong typography, no stock handshake photography, technical/geometric imagery over decorative graphics [^9] |
| Help users recognize and recover from errors | Booking-flow error states unspecified | Ensure the scheduler (e.g., Calendly-style embed) gives explicit confirmation, rescheduling, and fallback email options, following the boutique-agency booking pattern [^5] |
| Help and documentation | No FAQ or objection-handling content identified | Publish a short technical/trust FAQ addressing data privacy, consent for recording/transcription, and what happens after the call — directly answering the compliance risks Human Center has already flagged internally [^3] |

## Website Architecture Recommendation for Shrinking AI

Synthesising the McKinsey/Accenture-tier credibility architecture  with the boutique-agency booking pattern  and the emotional-trust requirements specific to the A4/A1 ICP, the recommended page structure is:[^6][^7][^9][^5][^3]

- **Homepage:** Fixed, permanent headline (not rotating, per Deloitte's flagged weakness) leading with outcome, not prestige — e.g., a variant of "The problem isn't the AI tool. It's the input. We fix the input in one session". Above-the-fold primary CTA: a single, unambiguous "Book your free 15-minute call" button, avoiding both BCG's zero-CTA flatness and Deloitte's five-CTA clutter.[^3][^2]
- **How it works / Engagement model page:** Explicit 3-phase visual (free empathy call → paid structured session → 2-hour deliverable → optional retainer), addressing the enterprise buyer's need to see process before committing, and reducing perceived risk.[^9]
- **Proof/Case evidence section:** Since case studies are still an open evidence gate, substitute an honest "early-stage, transparent" framing — a founder-led narrative and a visible before/after example of an AI instruction set — rather than fabricating claims, consistent with Human Center's own claim-discipline policy.[^3]
- **Trust/FAQ page:** Answering consent, data handling, and "will this feel like being sold to" objections directly and pre-emptively, mirroring the enterprise technical-FAQ pattern  but written in the plain, non-corporate, judgment-free register validated for this audience.[^9][^3]
- **Booking page:** Single embedded scheduler with qualification micro-form, following the boutique-agency benchmark exactly (AI Jungle, Benian, The Automators), with an email fallback and explicit expectation-setting copy ("15 minutes, no pitch, just a conversation about what's not working") drawn directly from the validated message variants.[^7][^5][^6][^3]
- **Separate, clearly distinguished section or subdomain for Product 2** (Career Development) to avoid heuristic "consistency and standards" violations from mixing B2B and B2C tone and CTAs on the same primary navigation.[^3]

## Key Usability Issues to Resolve Before Launch

- No qualification gate before booking risks wasted founder time on unqualified 30-minute calls, a named operational bottleneck.[^3]
- No visible proof assets yet (case studies, consent/compliance evidence) — the design must compensate with transparent process visuals rather than invented social proof.[^3]
- Risk of brand and message collision between the two product lines if navigation and CTAs are not strictly separated.[^3]
- Technical jargon must be avoided entirely in Product 1 copy per messaging guardrails; validated plain-language phrasing already exists and should be used verbatim where possible.[^3]
- CTA wording must balance enterprise credibility (avoiding overly informal "Get Started") with the approachable, non-judgmental tone this specific audience needs — "Book a free 15-minute conversation" tests better than generic "Request a Consultation" for this segment, based on the emotional-trust findings.[^9][^3]

## Opportunities for Differentiation

Shrinking AI's clearest strategic opportunity is to occupy the "human interaction quality layer" that sits between tool-level IT consulting and strategy-level change management — a positioning gap that no LinkedIn-identified competitor fills. A secondary opportunity is to become the only player in this niche that demonstrates its diagnostic value live on the website (e.g., an interactive mini-version of the empathy session or the Role-Context-Goal-Format-Constraints framework), mirroring the "demonstrated not claimed" advantage McKinsey's live AI chatbot holds over every other major consulting homepage evaluated. Finally, because the primary ICP is itself a consultant/freelancer segment, the site should support a light partner/referral mechanic (downloadable capability one-pager, referral-friendly case framing) that turns early adopters into a distribution channel, consistent with the "consultant marketplace" ambition already noted in internal planning.[^1][^2][^3]
