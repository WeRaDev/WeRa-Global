# Problem

## Purpose
Define the core problem Human Center's target customers face, as the basis for solution scoping.

## Scope
- In scope: knowledge-work back-office friction (email, documents, bookkeeping handoff, follow-ups) for solo/small professional-services businesses; the broader "AI productivity aggregation" problem of noisy human input degrading AI-driven productivity gains.
- Out of scope: enterprise-scale AI transformation programs; regulated industry-specific compliance automation beyond bounded back-office workflows.

## Current State
- Independent consultants and small professional-services firms accumulate high-friction, low-leverage manual workflows: email triage/drafting/follow-up, document/receipt processing, bookkeeping handoff, and task/follow-up tracking.
- Desk research identifies a broader, related phenomenon: individually productive AI usage does not reliably aggregate into organization- or business-level productivity gains, in part because of "emotionally dirty" or low-quality human input into AI systems.
- Some platform-design incentives may actively work against user productivity outcomes (engagement-optimized design vs. outcome-optimized design), a relevant risk to keep in mind when selecting/recommending tools for clients.

## Decisions / Rules
- Frame the problem narrowly per client (a specific bottleneck), not as generic "AI transformation," per Design Thinking / Theory of Constraints research.
- Do not assume the productivity-aggregation problem applies uniformly to every prospective client; validate per engagement.
