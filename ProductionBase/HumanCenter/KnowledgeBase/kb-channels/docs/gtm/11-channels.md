# Channels

## Purpose
Document validated acquisition-channel mechanics for Product 1 ("Shrinking AI").

## Scope
- In scope: the booking-page conversion mechanism and the consultant-referral channel hypothesis.
- Out of scope: paid acquisition/media strategy (not yet researched); Product 2 channels; Product 3 ("Automation Center") channels, see `12-automation-center-channels.md`.

## Current State
The primary conversion channel is the **website itself**, with a single goal: book a free 15-minute call. Boutique AI-agency booking pages reviewed (AI Jungle's "Book a 30-Min AI Workforce Call," Benian's "Book Your AI Discovery Call," The Automators' "free 30-minute discovery call") converge on a tactical pattern for lightweight B2B scheduling: a single embedded scheduler, one clear CTA, minimal form friction, and a plain-language outcome promise before the click. This is the tactical benchmark for Human Center's own booking mechanism (distinct from the strategic/credibility benchmark set by McKinsey-tier firms, which is for visual/tone credibility only, not conversion mechanics).

A secondary, longer-horizon channel hypothesis: because the primary ICP (A4 — AI-frustrated consultants/freelancers) is itself a segment capable of referring its own clients, a light partner/referral mechanic (a downloadable capability one-pager, referral-friendly case framing) could turn early adopters into a distribution channel — consistent with a "consultant marketplace" ambition already noted in internal planning. This channel is not yet built or tested.

## Decisions / Rules
- The booking page must follow the boutique-agency pattern (single scheduler, one CTA, qualification micro-form, email fallback) rather than the buried/multi-CTA pattern seen on McKinsey/Deloitte/BCG homepages.
- Add a short pre-booking qualification form (2-3 questions) before scheduling, to reduce wasted founder time on unqualified calls (a named operational risk).
