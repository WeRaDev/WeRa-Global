# Shrinking AI (B2B Consultancy) — Desk Research, Heuristic Evaluation \& Competitive Analysis

### For the Human Center website project · Primary goal: book 30-minute calls with potential clients


***

## Phase 1 — Desk Research: Market \& Problem Validation

**Research question:** *Validate the "AI adoption fails without human facilitation" problem for SME/consultant/freelancer buyers, in order to inform a Shrinking AI website whose single conversion goal is booking a 30-minute discovery call.*

### 1.1 Problem-validation statistics (fact — executed/observed, cross-validated 3+ sources)

| Finding | Figure | Sources |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Enterprise GenAI pilots failing to reach production / deliver P\&L impact | **95%** | MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" report, cited independently by Forbes[^1], Fortune[^2], Unframe[^3], AI in Time[^4] |
| Custom enterprise AI tools that ever reach production | **~5%** | MIT NANDA, corroborated by Fortune[^2] and AI in Time[^4] |
| Vendor/partner-led AI implementations succeed vs. internal builds | **67% vs. 33%** | MIT NANDA, repeated in Unframe[^3] and AI in Time[^4] — this is the strongest evidence for the "outside human facilitator beats DIY" thesis |
| EU AI adoption gap: large enterprises vs. small enterprises (10–49 employees) | **55% vs. 17%** (38-point gap) | Omago SME data brief[^5] |
| Non-adopting businesses citing "lack of expertise" as primary barrier | **50–71%** | Omago, aggregating EU/OECD/UK/G7 surveys[^5] |
| Organizations that fail to realize AI value due to disconnected workplace/workflow | **87%** | Appspace/Jacob Morgan webinar[^6] — hypothesis-adjacent, single-source, flag as **unverified** |
| Employees using AI vs. organizations translating it into real outcomes | 90% usage / **28%** outcome translation | EY report cited by ZDNet[^7] |
| Worker confidence in AI declining even as adoption rises | **-18% confidence, +13% adoption** | ManpowerGroup, via ZDNet[^7] |
| Leaders feeling pressure to prove AI ROI vs. actually tracking it | 61% pressured / only **20%** tracking | LinkedIn analyst post[^8] — single-source, flag as **unverified**, directionally consistent with EY/MIT data |
| Agentic AI projects predicted to be cancelled by 2027 | **40%+** | Gartner, via AI in Time[^4] |
| AI project failure rate vs. non-AI IT projects | **80%+ vs. ~2x lower** for non-AI | RAND Corporation, via AI in Time[^4] |

**Synthesis (fact):** The dominant, triple-sourced narrative is that AI failure is a **facilitation/expertise gap, not a technology gap** — and that external, vendor/partner-guided implementation roughly doubles success rates versus internal, unguided attempts. This is the single strongest evidentiary pillar for Shrinking AI's value proposition.

**Implication for the deliverable:** The hero section must lead with the "guided implementation beats DIY" statistic (67% vs 33%, or the 95%-fail framing), not a generic "AI can help your business" claim — buyers must self-identify as already having tried and failed before landing on the page.

### 1.2 Verbatim pain language (extracted, tagged by emotional tone)

- *"I lost two hours of my day"* — on AI tools creating more admin overhead than they saved (ZDNet headline, worker-reported) → tone: **fatigue/frustration**[^7]
- *"Not sure where to start with AI? You are not alone. Most UK SMEs know AI matters but struggle to separate genuine opportunity from hype."* — Hartz AI positioning copy → tone: **overwhelm, seeking permission to admit confusion**[^9]
- *"Before I did this course on AI prompting, I didn't see what all the fuss was about"* — client testimonial, Hartz AI → tone: **skepticism preceding relief**[^9]
- *"AI isn't failing because employees resist it... they don't trust it"* — LinkedIn thought-leadership framing → tone: **defensiveness/distrust**, reframes "resistance" as a trust/communication failure rather than laziness — buyers do not want to be blamed.[^10]

**Implication for the deliverable:** Copy must never imply the buyer or their team is "behind," "resistant," or "doing it wrong." The Hartz AI framing — *normalize confusion, then offer clarity* — is the correct emotional register.

### 1.3 Alternatives currently in use (and why they fail the buyer)

| Alternative | Why it fails |
| :-- | :-- |
| Self-serve tools (ChatGPT, generic SaaS AI features) | No context on the buyer's specific workflow; produces "digital graveyard" of disconnected point solutions[^6] |
| Internal DIY builds | Only 33% success rate vs. 67% for guided/vendor approaches[^3][^4] |
| Generic large-firm strategy consultants (McKinsey/Accenture-tier) | Priced and scoped for enterprise; multi-week engagement cycles; no SME/freelancer entry point |
| Trial-and-error via junior staff/interns | No accountability for outcomes measurement — echoes the "only 20% of leaders actually track ROI" gap[^8] |

**Implication:** Shrinking AI's positioning gap is directly between "too generic/self-serve" and "too enterprise/slow" — the underserved middle is SMEs, consultants, and freelancers who need fast, low-commitment, expert-guided clarity.

### 1.4 Competitor taxonomy (categories before company names)

1. **Aspirational/prestige strategy consultancies** — McKinsey, Accenture, BCG, Deloitte (brand-led, enterprise-scoped, contact buried)
2. **Boutique AI implementation agencies** — Hartz AI, Signal \& Form, Kresto Labs (SME-scoped, "book a call" CTA-led)
3. **Single-purpose discovery-call landing pages** — AI Jungle, Agentyis, ai-consultancy.company (ultra-narrow funnel, one CTA, no distractions)
4. **AI tool trainers / upskilling providers** — overlap with category 2, emphasize workshops over strategy
5. **Productivity/workflow coaches** — adjacent, not AI-specific, compete for the same "time-poor SME" attention

**Implication:** Shrinking AI should visually and tonally borrow the *prestige/credibility signaling* of category 1, but structurally and functionally copy the *conversion architecture* of categories 2–3.

## Phase 2 — Competitive Analysis

### 2.1 Benchmark set

**Aspirational tier:** McKinsey, Accenture, (reference only: BCG, Deloitte from prior research)
**Tactical tier:** Hartz AI, AI Jungle, Agentyis

### 2.2 Scoring rubric (1–5 scale)

| Axis | McKinsey | Accenture | Hartz AI | AI Jungle |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Positioning clarity | 2 (abstract: "What's your next brilliant move?") | 1 (pure slogan: "Juntos, Reinventamos") | 5 (states audience + problem + method in headline) | 5 ("Thirty minutes to map one workflow, test the fit") |
| CTA visibility above fold | 2 (arrow icon only, no label) | 1 (none visible) | 5 ("Book Discovery Call" button, dark contrast) | 5 (single green "Book a call" button, repeated) |
| Proof mechanisms | 4 (named case studies immediately below fold) | 3 (brand campaign, proof buried) | 4 (stats: 500+ trained, 90% confidence increase, named client logos + testimonials) | 3 (no stats yet, relies on tone/clarity) |
| Pricing transparency | 1 (never disclosed) | 1 (never disclosed) | 3 (states fractional CAO retainer "from £2,000/month" further down page) | 2 (not on this page; call itself is free) |
| Navigation structure | 3 (mega-menu, dense) | 3 (mega-menu, dense) | 4 (clear top nav: Training/Consultancy/Implementation/Governance) | 5 (minimal nav, one secondary CTA "Leverage Assessment") |
| Tone/register | 2 (grand, abstract, aspirational) | 1 (slogan-driven, impersonal) | 5 (plain language, empathetic: "You are not alone") | 5 (plain, precise, sets explicit fit criteria) |
| Mobile-first simplicity (inferred from layout density) | 2 | 2 | 4 | 5 |

### 2.3 Claims vs. delivery

- **McKinsey/Accenture**: Claim transformational, enterprise-scale impact; deliver via long sales cycles, named account teams, no visible self-serve entry point — inappropriate direct template for SME conversion, but excellent for *aspirational visual credibility* (typography, confident brevity, dark/light contrast).
- **Hartz AI**: Claims SME-specific, honest, non-hype consultancy; delivers via a phased "Audit → Roadmap → Quick wins → Scale" methodology, published pricing tier, and named testimonials — high trust signal, replicable structure.
- **AI Jungle**: Claims a single, low-commitment 30-minute assessment; delivers via a single-purpose landing page that explicitly states who should NOT book ("best when you know the work should create more leverage, but you do not yet know which workflow comes first") — this **explicit disqualification language is a strong trust and lead-quality signal**, rarely used elsewhere.


### 2.4 Positioning map (Technical depth vs. Human/emotional facilitation)

```
High technical depth   │  McKinsey/Accenture (enterprise strategy)
                        │  Kresto Labs / Agentyis (technical AI build)
                        │
                        │  ★ GAP: Shrinking AI ★
                        │  (human-input-quality layer, SME-scoped,
                        │   low-commitment entry point)
                        │
Low technical depth    │  Hartz AI, AI Jungle (training/workflow clarity)
                        └──────────────────────────────────────────
                          Low human/emotional facilitation → High
```

**Implication for the deliverable:** No competitor sits at the intersection of enterprise-level credibility signaling + SME-level low-commitment entry + high emotional/human facilitation. This is Shrinking AI's structural white space.
